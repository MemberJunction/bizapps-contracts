BizApps Contracts — UI direction, round 1

Four screens, built on the MJ design tokens and on bizapps-accounting's shell vocabulary (mj-page-body-interior, mj-page-header, mj-workspace-card with its tab strip and identity band, mj-stat-badge). Static HTML — nothing here is wired to anything.

The app's three jobs drive this layout. (1) Be the data substrate and pipeline automation that turns a filed deal into a contract and the contract into orders; (2) let the relevant people see a contract; (3) let an account director type one in by hand in the rare case it did not come from a deal. Screens 1 and 2 serve job 2, screen 3 serves job 3, and screen 4 is the operational surface of job 1 — the part of automation a human has to watch.
Job 2 · viewing

1 — Contracts list

The roster. Health of the book at the top (active value, renewing this quarter, billing due, failed events), then a filterable grid. The entry point for everyone who is not doing data entry.

 Job 2 · viewing

2 — Contract workspace

One agreement, section-tabbed: Overview · Terms · Coverage · Billing · Commitments · Amendments · History. Carries the provenance strip that answers "where did this come from, and where did it go."

 Job 3 · manual entry

3 — Manual contract entry

The mj-workspace-card pattern accounting uses for journal entries: browser-style draft tabs, an identity band, and a footer that owns commit / keep-as-draft / discard.

 Job 1 · automation

4 — Billing worklist

What the scheduled job is about to do, and what it could not do. Failed events are never auto-retried, so this screen is the only place a stuck bill becomes visible.

What this round is asking you to decide

  1. Section tabs vs. sub-pages for the contract detail. Tabs keep one URL and one loaded record; sub-pages deep-link better and let sales link straight to a term.
  2. Is the term the primary object? Screen 2 leads with the contract and nests terms. Terms carry the money and the dates, so leading with the active term and treating the contract as its header is a defensible alternative.
  3. Does the billing worklist belong to contracts or to orders? It shows contract billing events, but the thing a human fixes is usually an order/pricing problem. Same question as "who owns billing," one layer up.
  4. How loud should the provenance strip be? Deal → Contract → Order → JE is the whole pitch of the app; it is currently a quiet strip, and it could be the headline.

Honest status. Every screen is static HTML with invented data. Nothing is bound, no component exists yet, and the schema underneath is Amith's baseline on an-baseline-schema (PR #1), which is unmerged and has open questions against it. Where a screen shows something the schema cannot currently support, it says so in a grey callout rather than pretending.
