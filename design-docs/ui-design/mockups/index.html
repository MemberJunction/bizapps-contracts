BizApps Contracts — UI direction, round 1

Four screens, built on the MJ design tokens and on bizapps-accounting's shell vocabulary (mj-page-body-interior, mj-page-header, mj-workspace-card with its tab strip and identity band, mj-stat-badge). Static HTML — nothing here is wired to anything.

The app's three jobs drive this layout. (1) Be the data substrate and pipeline automation that turns a filed deal into a contract and the contract into orders; (2) let the relevant people see a contract; (3) let an account director type one in by hand in the rare case it did not come from a deal. Screens 1 and 2 serve job 2, screen 3 serves job 3, and screen 4 is the operational surface of job 1 — the part of automation a human has to watch.
This is its own UI, not orders wearing a hat. Contracts does not re-expose orders' order form, and it does not reimplement orders either. It is the place you record what was agreed — the customer, the products covered, the term and its clauses, and the subscription types and timelines those products become — and from that the backend creates the orders that flow to journal entries.

The division is about authority, not about screens: orders remains the only place anyone is billed and owns pricing, price history and discount mechanics, so nothing here recomputes money. But choosing a product, a subscription type, a coverage window, an escalation cap or a notice period is a contract decision, and it gets a first-class contract-native surface. Chrome follows the same restraint: a nav rail because there are five destinations, section tabs because one agreement genuinely has eight faces, and no draft-tab workspace on manual entry, because that task runs a few times a year.

Settled since round 1

  • Orders is the only place we bill. Contracts is the superset that consolidates; no billing groups in orders. So the billing worklist stays in this app.
  • A contract discount overrides whatever would apply beneath it in the order — it does not stack.
  • Documents use MJ's polymorphic file linking (__mj.FileEntityRecordLink), on contracts, terms and amendments alike. The DocumentFileID columns are gone.
  • Signatures use MJ's e-signature subsystem — __mj.SignatureRequest already carries EntityID/RecordID, so contracts and terms link to an envelope with zero columns and zero migration. Provider-agnostic (DocuSign · Dropbox Sign · PandaDoc, all seeded in core).
  • Status transitions are MJ Actions, not ad-hoc entity writes — the pattern orders already runs in this instance. PendingSignature is driven by the envelope, never set by hand.
  • No soft keys, ever — a mandate. Every cross-app reference is a real FK; the only non-FK pattern allowed is a genuine polymorphic EntityID/RecordID pair.

Still open — what this round asks

  1. Is the term the primary object? These screens lead with the contract and nest terms. Terms carry the money, the dates and every clause — leading with the active term and treating the contract as its header is a defensible alternative, and arguably truer now that terms carry their own paper.
  2. How much does Coverage decide? It now selects product + subscription type + timeline + price. The open question is where it stops — does the contract also pin things like trial periods and renewal lead days (both live on orders' Subscription), or does it set the type and let the type's own defaults carry them?
  3. Which AsOf does the engine pass on renewal — likely the individual subscription end dates (Andrew's call).
  4. Do <mj-record-files> and <mj-record-signature-status> get built here or donated to MJ base? Both are record-scoped and polymorphic, both are missing from MJ today, and every app that acquires paper or signatures wants them. Building them privately guarantees someone rebuilds them.

Honest status. Every screen is static HTML with invented data. Nothing is bound, no component exists yet, and the schema underneath is Amith's baseline on an-baseline-schema (PR #1), which is unmerged and has open questions against it. Where a screen shows something the schema cannot currently support, it says so in a grey callout rather than pretending.
